title = "Read side by side"
date = "2026-11-03"
summary = "Translations and notes follow the original's structure. If a sentence can be matched, the reading counts."
+++

If reading only keeps the bright sentences, they scatter afterwards. I would rather follow the original's sections, one passage against another. Later, looking it up, I can still step back into the author's order.

The Chinese translation of Writing Web Applications from the Go site was done that way: no reshuffle, no new path through the examples. Chinese sits against the original. A reader can open two windows.

Notes work the same. The pattern's name sits next to the implementation. A figure from the book sits next to a figure of my own. Comparison is more honest than paraphrase.

A note is useful when it can point back to a section.
